    About Jellyfish

    Ancient, mysterious, and endlessly fascinating — drifting through our oceans for over 500 million years.

    🌊

    What Are They?

    Jellyfish are soft-bodied, free-swimming marine animals with a gelatinous umbrella-shaped bell and trailing tentacles. They belong to the phylum Cnidaria and are among the oldest multicellular organisms on Earth.

    ⏳

    Ancient Existence

    Jellyfish have existed for over 500 million years — predating dinosaurs, sharks, and even trees. They have survived multiple mass extinction events and continue to thrive in every ocean on Earth.

    🌍

    Global Habitat

    Found in every ocean, from warm tropical waters to freezing Arctic depths. Some species even inhabit freshwater lakes and rivers, showcasing their incredible adaptability.

    ⚖️

    Ecosystem Role

    Jellyfish play a crucial role in marine food webs. They serve as food for sea turtles, sunfish, and seabirds, while their blooms can dramatically reshape ocean ecosystems.

    Scyphozoa True Jellyfish

    Explore the world of true jellyfish, their biology, habitats, and role in ocean ecosystems.

    What is Scyphozoa?

    Scyphozoa are the "true jellyfish" within the phylum Cnidaria. These animals are mainly marine and have a dominant medusa stage with a bell-shaped body, trailing tentacles, and a pulsing motion that carries them through the ocean.

    True jellyfish use stinging cells called nematocysts to capture prey and defend themselves.

    RankClassification
    PhylumCnidaria
    SubphylumMedusozoa
    ClassScyphozoa

    Body Structure

    Mostly water, with a gelatinous bell and trailing tentacles supported by rhythmic muscle contractions.

    Feeding Style

    Capture food with tentacles covered in nematocysts. Diet includes plankton, small fish, and crustaceans.

    Natural Habitat

    Live in oceans worldwide, from warm coastal waters to cold, deep seas.

    Common Species

    Moon jelly (Aurelia aurita), lion's mane (Cyanea capillata), compass jelly.

    Cubozoa Box Jellyfish

    Learn about box jellyfish, their unique anatomy, dangerous sting, and ocean habits.

    What is Cubozoa?

    Cubozoa are the box jellyfish, named for their cube-shaped bells. Known for fast swimming, complex eyes, and some of the most potent venom in the animal kingdom.

    Unlike most jellyfish, box jellyfish are powerful predators that actively hunt small fish and shrimp.

    RankClassification
    PhylumCnidaria
    SubphylumMedusozoa
    ClassCubozoa

    Anatomy

    Square bell with up to four corners, each carrying one or more clusters of long, venomous tentacles.

    Eyes

    24 eyes including lens-bearing camera eyes that can detect shapes, light, and obstacles.

    Habitat

    Warm, shallow coastal waters of the Indo-Pacific and Caribbean, especially near reefs and beaches.

    Danger

    Chironex fleckeri venom can cause severe pain, cardiovascular distress, and fatalities within minutes.

    Hydrozoa Hydrozoans

    Discover the diverse world of hydrozoans, from colonial man o' war to tiny freshwater hydra.

    What is Hydrozoa?

    Hydrozoa is a class of cnidarians that includes both solitary and colonial species. Many hydrozoans spend part of their lives as polyps attached to surfaces, while others form floating colonies.

    This group includes the Portuguese man o' war, as well as freshwater hydra and many tiny, delicate colonial forms.

    RankClassification
    PhylumCnidaria
    SubphylumMedusozoa
    ClassHydrozoa

    Life Cycle

    Often have alternating polyp and medusa stages. Some species form large colonies.

    Colonial Forms

    The Portuguese man o' war is made up of many specialised individuals working together as one floating organism.

    Freshwater Species

    Some hydrozoans, like hydra, live in freshwater habitats with impressive regenerative abilities.

    Feeding

    Capture prey with tentacles lined in nematocysts, feeding on plankton and small crustaceans.

    Staurozoa Stalked Jellyfish

    Learn about stalked jellyfish, their unusual body plan, and how they attach to the sea floor.

    What is Staurozoa?

    Staurozoa are stalked jellyfish that attach to surfaces using a stem-like structure. They look different from traditional free-swimming jellyfish and often live in cold, rocky waters.

    These unusual cnidarians are mostly sessile, staying fixed in one place while their arms capture food from passing water.

    RankClassification
    PhylumCnidaria
    SubphylumMedusozoa
    ClassStaurozoa

    Body Shape

    Bell-shaped body with arms around the top and a stalk that holds them to rocks or kelp.

    Habitat

    Usually found in cold coastal waters attached to seaweed, sponges, or hard surfaces.

    Feeding

    Use tentacles and adhesive pads to catch small crustaceans, plankton, and other tiny animals.

    Life Cycle

    Unlike many jellyfish, most stalked jellyfish do not have a free-swimming medusa stage.

    Jellyfish Anatomy

    Click each body part to reveal detailed information about jellyfish biology.

    Bell (Medusa)

    The umbrella-shaped body that pulses rhythmically to propel the jellyfish through water.

    The bell is composed of 95% water and contains the gastrovascular cavity for digestion. The rhythmic pulsing is powered by circular muscle fibres contracting at rates of 1–4 pulses per second.

    Tentacles

    Long, trailing appendages armed with thousands of microscopic stinging cells.

    Tentacles can extend several metres and contain nematocysts — specialised cells that fire microscopic harpoons at 40,000 G-forces to inject venom.

    Oral Arms

    Frilly structures surrounding the mouth that help capture and transport food.

    Oral arms capture prey and move it toward the mouth. In Scyphozoa they are typically four in number and can be heavily frilled, forming elaborate filtering structures.

    Nematocysts

    Specialised stinging organelles — the most sophisticated biological weapon in nature.

    Each nematocyst fires at 40,000 G-forces — one of the fastest mechanical events in all of biology. Discharged nematocysts cannot be reused; the cell is replaced.

    Mesoglea

    The gelatinous middle layer — the substance that makes jellyfish what they are.

    Mesoglea is 95–98% water, giving jellyfish their near-neutral buoyancy. When stranded on a beach, jellyfish rapidly evaporate leaving almost nothing behind.

    Gastrovascular Cavity

    The simple digestive chamber that serves as both stomach and circulatory system.

    A primitive digestive system with a single opening serving as both mouth and anus. It branches into canals that radiate through the bell, distributing nutrients directly to tissues.

    Life Cycle

    A remarkable journey of transformation — from microscopic egg to graceful adult medusa. Click any stage to watch a video.

    🥚 Egg

    Fertilised eggs develop into planula larvae within hours. Some species release eggs into the water while others brood them internally on the oral arms of the adult medusa.

    🐛 Planula Larva

    A free-swimming, ciliated larva that drifts for days before settling on a hard surface. It resembles a tiny flattened oval and moves using hair-like cilia beating in coordinated waves.

    🪸 Polyp (Scyphistoma)

    The larva attaches and transforms into a polyp — a stalk-like organism that feeds and grows. Polyps can reproduce asexually by budding, creating colonies that persist for years.

    ✨ Ephyra (Strobilation)

    Strobilation occurs: the polyp segments into disc-shaped ephyrae that break free one by one. Each ephyra is a miniature version of the adult jellyfish, just millimetres wide.

    🎐 Adult Medusa

    The ephyra grows into a mature medusa, completing the cycle. Some species, like the Immortal Jellyfish (Turritopsis dohrnii), can revert back to the polyp stage indefinitely.

    Fun Facts

    Mind-blowing truths about these ancient ocean drifters.

    01

    Prehistoric Survivors

    Jellyfish existed before dinosaurs, sharks, and even trees. They have survived for over 500 million years through multiple mass extinctions.

    02

    Biological Immortality

    The Immortal Jellyfish (Turritopsis dohrnii) can revert its cells back to their earliest form through transdifferentiation, potentially living forever under ideal conditions.

    03

    No Brain, No Heart

    Jellyfish have no brain, heart, bones, or blood. They use a decentralised nerve net to sense their environment.

    04

    95% Water

    A jellyfish's body is composed of 95% water. When washed ashore they evaporate within hours, leaving almost nothing visible behind.

    05

    Box Jellyfish Eyes

    Box jellyfish have 24 eyes including advanced camera-type eyes with lenses, retinas, and corneas — yet they have no centralised brain to process the images.

    06

    Fastest Sting on Earth

    Nematocysts fire at accelerations exceeding 5 million G — one of the fastest biological mechanisms ever recorded, faster than a bullet over the same distance.
